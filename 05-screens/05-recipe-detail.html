Recipe Detail

The screen that closes the loop (IA 5). A recipe here never dead-ends: the same “Log this” button that ends this screen opens the same Food Confirmation a barcode scan does, carrying the recipe’s figures and the serving count you set. That count is the question screens 03 and 04 deferred, and it is answered here: it means the servings you are eating. Built only from Design System v1.

  • Stage 5 · Screens, 05 of 05 · revision 2, after one critique
  • Phone frame: 390 × 844 with iPhone safe areas; the serving stepper works
  • Sample recipes and figures are illustrative, never real nutrition data

At a glance

Screen 5 of 5, and the one that closes IA structural rule 2: a recipe detail view never dead-ends. It ends in the same B1 button, in the same S3 action bar, opening the same Food Confirmation that a barcode scan opens — which is H4, the sharpest differentiator the research found, made structural rather than aspirational. It also settles the one decision screens 03 and 04 deferred: what a serving count means.

What the screen does

  • Shows the recipe: one image, the name, the yield, the nutrition, the ingredients and the method.
  • Puts the nutrition above the cooking, because you arrive still deciding whether to cook it.
  • Lets you set how many servings you are eating, and recalculates every figure live.
  • Keeps “Log this” in reach at every scroll position, and hands over exactly what the screen says.

Decided here

  • The serving count means the servings you are eating, and it carries into Food Confirmation. Full argument in the next section.
  • The yield is information, not a control. “Makes 4 servings” under the title, and “For all 4 servings” over the ingredients.
  • The hero reuses ratio-recipe-image. One crop at three sizes — thumbnail, card, hero — rather than a second ratio for the same dish.
  • Ingredients are figures on Paper; the method is words on Oat. The unifying rule at the scale of a section.

Still open

  • Audit A9: whether 4:3 is the right crop. Screen 04 answered placement; the proportion closes at the Krea pass, and now has a third size riding on it.
  • Ingredient quantities are illustrative, and are not the source of the nutrition figures. Where a recipe’s nutrition comes from is undecided (PRODUCT.md).
  • Saving, sharing, rating, scaling the recipe itself: all out of the IA 5 content list.
  • The fit that brought you here is not restated, and a bigger portion never says it has broken that fit. One v2 item shared with screen 03, argued below.

What a serving count means

Screen 03 built the recipe path with this question open, and wrote it down rather than guessing: “If IA 5’s stepper means ‘servings I’m eating’, it carries over; if it means ‘servings the recipe makes’, Confirmation opens at 1.” Screen 04 carried it forward. It has to be answered here, because this is the screen with the stepper on it.

Decision: the count is the servings you are eating. It carries into Food Confirmation, which opens on the number this screen was left showing. The recipe’s own yield is shown as a fact and cannot be changed.

What this gives up, and what it doesn’t

  • You cannot scale the recipe here. A cook feeding six can’t ask this screen to rewrite the quantities. That is a real loss for Segment 2, and it is out of the IA’s content list, so it is recorded rather than smuggled in. It would be a v2 feature with its own control and its own name (“cook for …”), never the same stepper.
  • The yield still has to be visible, or the ingredient list and the per-serving figures don’t connect: a reader can’t tell what one serving of 400 g of chickpeas is. It appears twice, as a fact both times — “Makes 4 servings” under the title and “For all 4 servings” over the ingredients. Judgment call: the IA lists neither.
  • Confirmation still owns the final say. Flow 3 says “adjust serving if needed” there, and PRODUCT’s rule that a logged entry is never final means it can be changed again from Today. Setting the count here is a head start, not a commitment.
  • One control, two places. Both screens use the same I3 stepper, the same unit word and the same step of 0.5, so the handoff reads as one control that followed you rather than two that agree.
  • Half servings matter. Stepping down from 1 gives 0.5, and the block renames itself “For half a serving”. Research says what people eat rarely matches the serving on the label.

Left offWhy
Cook time, difficulty, ratings, reviews, a author or source bylineNone is in the IA 5 content list, and screen 04 left the same fields off its cards for the same reason. Adding them here would also mean a results card can’t show them, which is where they would actually help a choice.
Saving, favouriting, adding to a meal plan, sharing, a shopping listAll are real recipe-app features and none serves either user story. “Generic un-filtered recipe browsing” is explicitly out of scope in 04-conclusions.md, and a saved-recipe library is the same kind of thing.

Heuristics, applied while building

Nielsen’s ten heuristics served as constraints during generation; the single /impeccable critique run tested them afterwards.

HeuristicApplied as
1 · Visibility of system statusThe block label always names what the figures describe, and changes the moment the count does. The count itself is a number you can read, not a slider position. Screen readers get one announcement after typing pauses, not one per keystroke.
2 · Match with the real world“Makes 4 servings”, “Servings you’re eating”, “For all 4 servings, as the recipe is written”, “For half a serving”. A cook’s words, not a data model’s.
3 · User control and freedomNothing here commits: Back returns to the results as they were, the count can be changed again on Confirmation, and a logged entry can still be corrected from Today (Flow 4).
4 · Consistency and standardsThe same nutrition block, the same stepper, the same action bar and the same primary button as the logging screens. A figure looks identical here and on Today. The back-versus-close rule is the one screen 03 set.
5 · Error preventionThe main error this screen could cause is logging the wrong amount, so the amount is named in words above the figures and again inside the button’s accessible name. An empty or unreadable count restores the last valid one instead of showing stale figures.
6 · Recognition over recallThe yield sits beside the ingredients it explains, so nobody has to remember it while reading quantities. Limit: the fit that brought you here (“170 kcal under your limit”) is not repeated on this screen — a deliberate choice, since those margins belong to the filter you set, and restating them here would mean restating a filter you can’t see or change from this screen.
7 · Flexibility and efficiencyA user eating one serving touches nothing at all: the screen opens at 1 and “Log this” is already correct. A half portion is one tap.
8 · Aesthetic and minimalist designThree controls, five blocks, one image. No ratings, no badges, no micronutrient table, no disclosure hiding the numbers.
9 · Recognize and recover from errorsThere is no failure state on this screen — the recipe is already loaded — so the work is in the next screen: “Log this” leads to a review step, never straight to a logged entry.
10 · Help and documentationOne line, in the one place the screen could be misread: over the ingredients, saying what they are measured for.

Detector findings

17 findings, 5 rules, none real. False positives (16): cramped-padding ×13 — nine on the bay, the device frames, the notes sections and the annotation pills, all below the “not part of the screen or the system” marker, and four on .actionbar, which is product UI but computes padding: 12px 20px 46px; the scanner does not resolve the var(). all-caps-body ×1, on an 11 px annotation pill in these notes (uppercase inside a .screen is only the 24-character placeholder label and the ≤16-character overlines). flat-type-hierarchy ×1, because the scanner cannot read the var(--type-*) shorthands; measured in the browser the scale runs 48 / 32 / 22 / 16 / 14 / 12 with a largest adjacent step of 1.50:1. cream-palette ×1, which is Paper, a Stage 3 brand colour. Deliberate (1): the M1 placeholder stripes. Screen 05 introduces no new rule against screens 02–04, and drops the aphoristic-cadence matches that fired on 03 and 04. No user-visible overlay was produced: that needs script injection into the deliverable, and this was a read-only pass, so every measurement came from getBoundingClientRect and getComputedStyle.

The question the critique asked that has no cheap answer. “Name the warm words that are yours rather than the recipe author’s.” On the screen where the brand says warmth is allowed to be loudest, the product’s own voice amounts to “Back”, “Makes 4 servings”, “Per serving”, “Servings you’re eating”, “Ingredients”, “Method” and “Log this”. That is a fair hit, and it is not a bug to patch with adjectives — the Stage 3 tone guardrail is “calm, honest, warm, exact”, and warmth bought by adding words to a screen a cook reads mid-task would cost the exactness. Recorded as the open question it is, for the copy pass this project never scheduled.
