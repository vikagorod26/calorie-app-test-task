Today / Daily Log

The home screen, and the place both stories return to (IA 1). At a glance it answers how much is left today. It lists every entry with where its number came from, lets you correct any entry, and keeps Add food one tap away. Built only from Design System v1.

  • Stage 5 · Screens, 01 of 05 · revision 2, after critique
  • Phone frame: 390 × 844 with iPhone safe areas; scroll inside the frame
  • Sample figures are illustrative, never real nutrition data

At a glance

This is revision 2. Revision 1 scored 23/40 in /impeccable critique. Revision 2 scored 25/40 in a second, independent run, which led to two more changes: a shared meter scale, then a source-first meta line in every row. Every decision the notes had already documented was kept.

What the screen does

  • Shows what’s left today, with the arithmetic behind it in plain view.
  • Lists every entry, newest first, each with the source of its number.
  • Opens any entry for correction, and keeps Add food and Recipes one tap away.

Changed in revision 2

  • Entries open Food Confirmation to fix or remove them (new IA Flow 4).
  • “Over” reads at a glance: bold word, a target tick on every meter, macros in words.
  • Macros answer the question directly: “50 g left”.
  • Each entry leads with where its number came from.
  • Works down to 195 px wide (200% zoom on a 390 px phone).
  • A misleading label removed; details and page fixes listed below.

Still open

  • Undo straight after “Log it” and the empty day: v2 states.
  • Where the 2,000 target comes from: profile is out of scope (IA).
  • OS text-size settings: native build maps type to pt / sp.
  • From run 2: a dense summary, an ungrouped list, and row names still to confirm with a real screen reader.

Heuristics, applied while building

Nielsen’s ten heuristics served as design constraints during generation, and the /impeccable critique runs tested them afterwards.

HeuristicApplied as
1 · Visibility of system statusThe hero says what’s left or how far over, with the weight change and overflow visible at a glance. The caption shows eaten and target. The newest entry sits on top; the current tab is marked by fill, line, weight and aria-current.
2 · Match with the real worldEveryday words: “left”, “eaten”, “target”, “over”, “70 of 120 g”. Clock times, kcal and grams. Sources named in words.
3 · User control and freedomAny entry opens for correction or removal (Flow 4). Today and Recipes stay one tap apart. Undo right after logging comes with v2 states.
4 · Consistency and standardsCalories and macros both state what’s left. A log entry, a search result and a recipe entry share one row (C2). Add food is the same B1 as “Log it” and “Log this”.
5 · Error preventionNo signed numbers such as “−47”. Every row names its source, so a doubtful entry stands out before it skews the day.
6 · Recognition over recallTargets are printed beside what’s eaten, and macro answers need no arithmetic. Tabs and Add food carry text labels; the list label states the order.
7 · Flexibility and efficiencyAdd food is one tap from Today and Recipes, in thumb reach at every width. Quick re-log stays out of scope (04-conclusions.md §2).
8 · Aesthetic and minimalist designOnly IA 1’s content; a redundant label removed; nothing sits above the figures.
9 · Recognize and recover from errorsA wrong entry can be corrected or removed from where it’s noticed. Empty and error states follow in v2.
10 · Help and documentationThe caption shows the arithmetic behind “left”. The target’s origin sits in the out-of-scope profile.

Images: none on this screen, on purpose

Placeholder space is reserved wherever the IA or the system calls for an image. For Today, neither does, and adding one would break a documented rule.

  • IA Flow 3: a logged recipe appears “identical in form to a manually-logged item”, so recipe rows can’t carry a thumbnail the others lack.
  • Hybrid §3: IA 1 imagery is “None”. §7 places the small recipe thumbnail “when arriving from a recipe”, which is Food Confirmation (IA 3); tokens §4 gives that thumbnail radius-sm.
  • Where image slots will appear: the IA 3 thumbnail (Flow 3 only), IA 4 card images (4:3, provisional) and the IA 5 hero.
  • How they’ll be marked: a striped block labelled “IMAGE · UNVALIDATED (§7)”, following 03-branding/03-direction-preview.html. It gets rebuilt from tokens and added to the system when IA 3 needs it; real images arrive in the Krea pass.

Checks run on this file

  • Verbatim system CSS, checked at build. Component CSS has no raw px, hex, size or weight values except the two documented @container breakpoints.
  • Rendered at 390 px: 0 colors outside the palette, 31 of 31 figures tabular, one visible serif element per frame. Every visible font combination maps to a role token.
  • Touch targets, at 390 / 320 / 300 / 195 px: tabs 94 / 59 / 68 / 60 wide; Add food 147 (“Add food”) / 147 / 107 (“Add”) / 52 (icon). Everything is 52 px tall, and entry rows span the full width.
  • Breakpoints measured: the bar needs 309 px with “Add food”, 270 px with “Add” and 182 px icon-only, hence switches at 320 and 280.
  • Reflow: no horizontal scrolling and no bar overlap at 390, 320, 300 or 195 px. Meta segments never split, and at 195 px the hero unit and the kcal figures move to their own lines.
  • Details: hero number-to-unit gap 9 px (was about 13); 13 entry links whose accessible names end in “Edit or remove”.
  • Shared meter scale, measured at 390 px: the target tick sits at 277 of 318 px (87%) on all 8 meters. In frame B, protein ends at 254 and carbs at 269, short of the tick; calories stop at the tick with a 6 px hatch past it, and fat’s hatch runs to 313. Nothing spills past a track at 390 or 195 px.
  • Typeset pass, frame B: the source sits on line 1 in 8 of 8 rows at 390 and 320 px (before: 7 of 8 wrapped at 320 px) and never splits. It measures 14 px / 500 / 15.51:1 with its icon; amount digits 14 / 500 / 15.51:1 tabular with the unit at 6.27:1; time 14 / 400 / 6.27:1. Cost: at 390 px, 5 of 8 rows now put amount and time on a second line (list 564 → 668 px). Accepted: trust in the logging method outranks list compactness. A tighter-gap variant was rejected because “Food database 150” reads as one phrase; density stays with N3.
  • Not verified: a full screen-reader walkthrough (the two review tools disagreed on row names), and OS text-size settings (audit E6).
