Visual direction moodboards

No direction is selected here. This page is input for that decision. Full notes and citations are in 01-direction-options.md.

Direction A

Clinical Ledger

A spreadsheet in a phone: white grouped lists on light gray, hairline dividers, and every number right-aligned in its own column. Color is used almost only for meaning: one saturated blue for actions and totals, plus small tinted bars for macros. It reads as institutional, complete and auditable, never decorative.

  • iOS system sans (SF Pro) throughout. Hierarchy comes from weight and gray, not size.
  • Numerals flush right in columns, so they scan like a ledger.
  • Text buttons in blue all caps with slight tracking. Only the dashboard uses a large bold title.

Pros for this project

  • H2 trust = accuracy: the most transparent idiom. Visible arithmetic and full nutrient tables let Segments 1 & 3 "trust my numbers without redoing the math".
  • IA "logging one action away": the docked search + barcode bar is exactly the always-reachable entry point research calls for.
  • H4 precedent: a recipe detail that resolves into a sticky "Log to diary" button, so "a recipe is never a dead end" already works in practice.
  • Cheapest to systematize (system type, one accent), which fits "minimize manual work".

Cons for this project

  • It is the incumbent's look. Segment 1 are switchers burned by MyFitnessPal, and the dashboard literally shows a PREMIUM crown (paywalling is the loudest betrayal signal, H2).
  • Weak for H3: gray nutrient tables give home cooks (Segment 2) no appetite or confidence to cook, and they already leave for recipe blogs.
  • "Chore" risk (H1): long gray lists and small caps links make daily logging feel like bookkeeping.
  • All four references come from one app, so it's partly a house style with little room to differentiate.
Direction B

Oat & Serif

Calm, magazine-like wellness. Warm oat and cream grounds replace white, cards are barely lighter than the page and float on soft shadow, and there's a lot of empty space around one question at a time. Color comes from nature (leaf and pine greens, a grainy gradient) with a single warm coral accent. It feels like a cookbook more than a tool.

Maintain weight
  • High-contrast transitional serif, regular weight and centered, carries the voice. The wordmark serif has a leaf i-dot.
  • Controls switch to a clean geometric sans in semibold.
  • Small, widely tracked all-caps section labels ("FOOD PREFERENCES"). Noom uses a light tracked grotesk.

Pros for this project

  • Segment 2 & H3: "eating right for me" and confidence before cooking, in couch or commute planning moments. A cookbook tone is the most natural host for recipes as a first-class section.
  • IA "profile of me set once": the dietary-needs screen is almost exactly the diet-tag + allergy profile that feeds targets and recipe filters (P0 #5, P1 #6).
  • Distance from the clinical-blue incumbents without going gimmicky.
  • Serif + sans pairing gives an ownable voice from a small kit.

Cons for this project

  • H1 speed: cards #FCF6F6 on #F0EAE4 are nearly 1:1 contrast, held by shadow only, and airy layouts slow the one-handed in-the-moment log.
  • H2 trust in numbers: the serif, soft-wellness tone can read as approximate, and there's no reference for dense macro UI in this style, so it would have to be invented.
  • Paywall association: Lifesum's subscription screen (seen, not used) lists "Free" last, so copying Lifesum wholesale borrows that reputation.
  • Cliché risk: beige + green + serif is a crowded wellness look, so differentiation must come from the product (H4).
Direction C

Bold Coach

Confident and motivational: a stark black-and-white base (black pill buttons, black feature cards) punctuated by one warm energetic hue and occasional character illustration. Numbers and headlines are big and heavy, so they read at arm's length. It's a coach cheering you on rather than a ledger or a cookbook.

  • Ultra-heavy, tightly spaced display grotesque (Yazio). Bold rounded grotesk in amber (Cal AI).
  • MacroFactor pairs large light-weight numerals with a smaller unit, so numbers read as precise without looking clinical.
  • Bold white-on-black pill buttons, plain humanist body copy.

Pros for this project

  • H1 glanceable, one-handed: max contrast + oversized numerals answer "does this fit my day?" in a second, and one black pill = one obvious action, a clean base for "log this" (H4).
  • Segment 1 "visible, measurable progress": momentum cues speak to the payoff trackers cite.
  • Data can be bold: big light numerals + coral range chart give Segment 3 precision without A's spreadsheet feel.
  • Strongest distance from blue-list incumbents, and the most memorable brand.

Cons for this project

  • Outside the audience scope: the 5W segments explicitly exclude gamification, and tips and streaks are engagement surfaces, not the core loop.
  • H2 trust: a mascot and celebratory tone can undercut perceived accuracy. Cal AI's photo estimates were a top scrutiny point in research.
  • H3: the mono black/white base does nothing for appetite, so food imagery would fight a stark UI.
  • Production cost: custom illustration contradicts "minimize manual work", and a mascot-free variant loses much of the personality.

What the comparison shows (judgment call, not a selection): no single reference direction covers both user stories. A and C serve story 1 and B serves story 2, which mirrors research's finding that incumbents treat recipes as secondary. The choice is which weakness is cheaper to fix inside the design system: warming up a numerically rigorous base, or sharpening a warm one with a high-contrast data layer.
Known gap: none of the 12 screens shows food photography or recipe browsing, which matches H3 (recipes aren't a headline feature for incumbents). Recipe surfaces will need to be designed without a close reference in this set.
