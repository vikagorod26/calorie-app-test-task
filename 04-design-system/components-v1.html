Design System · Stage 7

The Measured Kitchen, as a system

A living reference, not a Stage 4 snapshot — built as the v1 foundation, then extended in place through Screens, interaction states (Stage 6) and real imagery (Stage 7) without a separate "final" version. A working reference for the five IA screens: tokens first (color, type roles, spacing, radius), then default-state components built only from semantic tokens, now with interaction states for the components that need them. The text spec lives in tokens-v1.md, and the CSS on this page uses the same token names.

  • States: buttons, chips, inputs and the stepper now cover hover/pressed/disabled/error/loading (§11, Stage 6). Everything else is still default-state only.
  • In sync: the system CSS on this page is byte-identical in the five screens (05-screens/) and the clickable prototype (06-prototype/).
  • Specimens at phone width (390 px) on the Oat ground.
  • Sample figures are illustrative, not real nutrition data.
  • Audited: audit-v1.md

Foundation 1

Two tiers. Primitives keep the stylescape’s friendly names. Semantic roles say what a color does, and components only ever use roles. The brand palette is unchanged: Oat Deep is formalized from the stylescape CSS, and Stone is a new functional neutral for control edges.

Usage rules

  1. Pine means “act” or “chosen.” Neutral tappables (unselected chips, fields) are identified by a Stone edge, never by another hue.
  2. Selection = Pine Light fill + Pine line (+ check on chips). Labels stay in Ink, so a figure inside a chip is never tinted.
  3. Macro colors are marks. Tracks are Oat, and adjacent segments get a 2 px gap.
  4. No reward, premium or alarm hues. Over-target gets no color (see J1).
  5. Figures are never tinted, faded or italic, estimates included (see J2).

Foundation 4

Radius

Shape follows role. Things you choose are pills. Things that act or take input are rounded rectangles. A selected chip and a primary button can both carry pine, and shape keeps them apart at a glance.

Decisions

Two judgment calls

Stage 3 left both open. Research doesn’t say how users want either framed, so each is derived from the Stage 3 rules: no reward colors, not gamified, figures checkable rather than promised accurate. Both were then tested on the real screens: J1 was revised when its revisit condition was met, and J2 held as specified. Full reasoning is in tokens-v1.md §5.

J1Judgment call

Over target is information, not an alarm

No status hue. The hero keeps its treatment and changes its word from “left” to “over”. The meter goes solid Ink to the target, then a size-separator gap, then a hatched overflow. Revised at Screens: “over” is set in Ink at 600, every meter marks its target with a tick at the same position (meter-target), and macros say “12 g over” in words.

Why

  • “Over” isn’t bad for everyone. Our users gain weight and train as well as lose (PRODUCT.md, Segment 1). An alarm would misread many of them.
  • Brand tone: “No confetti, no guilt trips” (stylescape). Red is a guilt trip, and green-for-under is a reward color.
  • L1: a red number is a tinted figure.
  • The word and the hatch carry the meaning, so it works without color.

Rejected

  • Red or terracotta figure/meter (alarm tone; Terracotta already means protein)
  • Amber “warning” state (a new status hue)
  • “−180” (ambiguous next to “left”)
  • Celebrating staying under (reward mechanics)

  • Met at Screens (IA 1): “over” was too easy to miss on Today. Fixed with weight, a shared meter scale and a target tick; still no color. A second review found bold “over” alarming on a small overage; kept by decision.

Decisions

Remaining backlog

Struck items are done, with the stage that closed them. The rest is genuinely open.

  • Interaction states for buttons, chips, inputs and the stepper: hover, pressed, focus rendering, disabled, loading. Done, Stage 6 — see §11. Error and empty states for other components (cards, the nutrition panel) and a broader loading vocabulary (skeletons, camera-mode feedback) are still open.
  • Whether an error color is needed at all. Resolved, Stage 6: yes, for I2 and I3 (see §11.4) — always distinct from J1's over-target, which stays hue-free by design.
  • Elevation. v1 separates by spacing and hairlines. A sticky action bar on IA 3/5 may need one shadow token.
  • Motion beyond the loading spinner, e.g. results updating in place on IA 4. (The one exception, a loading spinner, shipped at Stage 6 — see §11.5 — because it's functional, not decorative, and respects prefers-reduced-motion.)
  • Camera-mode UI. Done, Stage 5 (Add Food): the viewfinder (V1) and the capture button (B1 in S3); a disabled mode segment for a denied permission followed at Stage 6 (§11). Still open: the full camera-permission flow, "no barcode found" and live detection feedback.
  • Imagery tokens (grade, final crops). Resolved, Stage 7: all 16 M1 slots carry real dish images; ratio-recipe-image (4:3) is confirmed and audit A9 closed. No grade token — the grade lives in the images.
  • Dark mode: open, and not planned for this deliverable. The brand commits to one warm, light look.
  • A clickable prototype. Done, Stage 8 / 8b: 06-prototype/, both flows wired, audited 18/20, carrying this system's CSS unchanged.
