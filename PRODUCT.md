## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS prototypes, delivered in `/05-screens`. Screens are rendered at phone viewport sizes and stand in for the target mobile app (see Capabilities and Constraints).

## Users

- **Primary: goal-driven trackers.** People losing or gaining weight, or training, who count calories (and often macros) every day. Their job: get an accurate calorie number for what they eat, fast, as part of a daily routine.
- **Secondary: home cooks.** People cooking at home who want to know how many calories a dish they make contains and to discover new recipes that suit them.
- **Bridge segment: macro-conscious meal planners.** People who plan meals around specific macro targets, want recipes matched to those targets (not just diet tags), and expect to log the recipe with the same precision once cooked. Identified in `/01-research/02-audience-5w.md` as underserved by existing apps — see "Suitable for me" definition below.

## Product Purpose

A calorie calculator app covering two user stories from the brief:

1. As a user, I want to calculate the amount of calories in a dish or a specific product.
2. As a user, I want to find a recipe for a dish that is suitable for me.

Context: this is a test task for a UX/UI Trainee Designer position. Success means a coherent, research-backed flow for both stories, a systemic brand-first design system, and final screens produced through an AI-native workflow with minimal manual work.

## Positioning

**Resolved** after Stage 1 research and Stage 3 branding.

A calorie tracker that also finds recipes that genuinely suit you, and joins the two into one flow: find a recipe that fits your diet and your numbers, then log it with the same accuracy you planned it with.

- **Differentiator:** recipe discovery filtered by both diet tags and macro targets, plus a recipe-to-log handoff that carries the recipe's nutrition straight into a logged entry. These are the two Delighter opportunities in `/01-research/03-competitor-analysis.md` and hypotheses H3–H4 in `/01-research/04-conclusions.md`; none of the three trackers studied (MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, Cal AI) does either well.
- **Table stakes, done without friction or gating:** fast logging by search, barcode or photo, with macros and personal targets (P0 features, H1). Core logging never feels locked away (H2).
- **Brand expression:** The Measured Kitchen — see Brand Commitments and `/03-branding/02-hybrid-direction.md`.
- **Guardrails:** don't promise accuracy the product can't guarantee (AI-photo results are estimates); the monetization model is still undecided (see Capabilities and Constraints). The one-line positioning wording above is a synthesis of the cited findings, not a quoted research result.

## Operating Context

- Trackers use the app repeatedly throughout the day, often mid-meal-prep or right before or after eating; speed of entry matters more than depth.
- Home cooks use it in the kitchen while planning or cooking a dish, moving between a recipe and its calorie breakdown.
- The work is evaluated as a hiring test task: reviewers read the process (research → IA → branding → design system → screens) as well as the final screens.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Target platform:** cross-platform mobile app (iOS + Android) with one shared, brand-first design language. It does not strictly follow iOS HIG or Material Design 3; native conventions are respected only where they matter (safe areas, system back behavior, touch target sizes, now fixed as the 48 px `size-hit-min` token). Recorded as `web` above because deliverables are HTML/CSS prototypes and the design language is not per-OS native.
- **In scope:** calorie calculation for a single product and for a composed dish (logged via search, barcode scan or AI photo); recipe discovery filtered to what suits the user; the recipe-to-log handoff. The screen set is the five screens in `/02-information-architecture/information-architecture.md` (Today / Daily Log, Add Food, Food Confirmation & Log, Recipes & Filter, Recipe Detail). Onboarding and Profile/Settings screens are out of scope for this deliverable.
- **A logged entry is never final** (added at Screens, after the Today critique; IA Flow 4 and structural rule 3). Every entry on Today is a link to Food Confirmation & Log. It opens pre-filled, with "Save changes" and "Remove entry" in place of "Log it". So Food Confirmation has three ways in: a fresh item from Add Food, a recipe from Recipe Detail ("Log this", Flow 3), and an existing entry from Today (Flow 4). Evidence: barcode, photo and database accuracy findings and H2. Reusing one screen is a flagged judgment call.
- **A recipe's serving count means the servings you are eating** (resolved at Screens, IA 5), and it carries into Food Confirmation, which opens on the number Recipe Detail was left showing. The recipe's own yield ("Makes 4 servings") is shown as information and cannot be changed. **The deciding argument:** the other reading — "servings the recipe makes" — would have to rescale every ingredient quantity, which IA 5 never asks for and no research finding supports; only this reading leaves the ingredient list alone, as the IA describes it. Supported by IA Flow 3 (what is carried must be what the screen showed), Segment 3's job story, MyFitnessPal's "save-and-log a serving" pattern, and the Cronometer finding that label servings are not what people eat. **Cost, recorded:** a cook cannot scale the recipe here; that would be a separate v2 control with its own name, never this stepper.
- **Language:** all deliverables must be in English.
- **Process constraint:** minimize manual work; keep screen generation within a single session to avoid visual drift.
- **"Suitable for me" is defined as BOTH of the following, not either/or** (resolved via `/01-research/02-audience-5w.md`):
  1. **Dietary/goal-based fit** — simple diet tags and constraints (e.g. vegan, low-carb, keto, allergies) — serves the home-cook segment, who thinks in terms of "does this fit how I eat," not exact numbers.
  2. **Macro-precision fit** — matching a recipe against specific calorie/macro targets — serves the macro-conscious meal-planner segment, identified in research as an underserved audience sitting between the two user stories (they want to find a recipe that hits their numbers, then log it with the same precision, not reverse-engineer the math by hand).
  Recipe filtering/search must support both simultaneously (tag-based filters and macro-target matching), since the two segments select recipes on different criteria and neither is a subset of the other.
  **Settled at Screens (IA 4, after the critique): a filter mode switches which criteria you *tune*, never which ones *apply*.** Revision 1 let switching to macro match drop every saved diet criterion except allergies, which showed a vegetarian meat dishes — the fastest possible way to lose the trust the screen exists to win. Every saved criterion now holds in both modes and the results label restates all of them in both.
- **A dish's kcal total is the source's own figure**, not a sum of its macros, so the two can legitimately differ by a few kcal (rounding, fibre, alcohol). Screens present the total as the source gives it rather than recomputing it (corrected in the Food Confirmation notes).
- **Still undecided:** data sources for nutrition values; where saved targets and diet preferences come from (onboarding/profile sit outside the five-screen set); account/sync features; and the monetization model (research only requires that core logging never feels gated). Over-target tone and AI-photo estimate display were decided in Design System v1 as provisional judgment calls (`/04-design-system/tokens-v1.md` §5, J1–J2), to revisit on real screens.

## Brand Commitments

Set in Stage 3 and **binding** for the Design System and Screens stages. Any deviation must be justified against research or the brief and reflected back into `/03-branding/04-stylescape.html` and `/03-branding/02-hybrid-direction.md`.

- **Concept: The Measured Kitchen.** A working brand concept combining a ledger's numerical trust with a cookbook's warmth. A final product name has not been set.
- **Unifying rule: "Words are warm, figures are exact."** Every nutrition figure gets one identical treatment on logging and recipe screens alike; warmth lives in words, ground and imagery, never in the data; contexts shift emphasis, not tokens.
- **Palette:** Oat `#F2EDE6` (ground) · Paper `#FFFDF9` (data surfaces) · Ink `#1F2421` (text and every figure) · Pine `#2F5245` (the only action color) · Pine Light `#DCE6DF` (a chosen diet tag or macro-match filter) · macro marks Terracotta `#B0513A` (protein), Ochre `#A87A1F` (carbs), Slate `#4D6A85` (fat), used as marks only with labels in ink.
- **Typography:** Newsreader for voice (recipe names, headlines); Inter for interface and all figures, with tabular numerals. Numbers are never set in the serif. **Both families are vendored locally** at `/assets/fonts/` (Stage 7b, SIL OFL 1.1) and linked from every HTML deliverable, so the typography survives an offline reviewer, a private window or a Google Fonts block — the failure mode that would otherwise have degraded the brand rule silently. Variable fonts, so Newsreader keeps its optical-size axis. The `--font-voice` / `--font-ui` fallback stacks were verified to take over cleanly if the local files are ever missing.
- **Voice and tone:** calm, honest, warm, exact. Not clinical, not gamified: no streaks, mascots, custom illustration, or reward/premium colors.
- **Copy guardrails:** present figures as consistent and checkable, not guaranteed accurate; "core logging never feels locked away"; the "no words over the plate" imagery rule applies to recipe imagery, not photo-scan UI.

## Product Principles

1. **Both stories are first-class.** Calorie calculation and recipe discovery are equal entry points, not a main feature plus an afterthought.
2. **Fast for daily use.** Trackers repeat the calculation flow many times a day; minimize steps and typing to a number.
3. **Personal fit over generic lists.** "Suitable for me" should visibly reflect the user's own goals and constraints.
4. **Research before decisions.** Branding and UX choices trace back to evidence in `/01-research/`.
5. **Systemic, not screen-by-screen.** Every screen is assembled from the shared design system.
